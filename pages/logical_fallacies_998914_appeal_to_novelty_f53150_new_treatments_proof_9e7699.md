## Introduction

The [appeal to novelty]({{ 'novelty/' | relative_url }}) becomes especially consequential in medicine because the stakes are unusually high. A new drug, device, or procedure can be genuinely innovative, yet innovation alone does not show that patients will live longer, feel better, function better, or [experience]({{ 'experience/' | relative_url }}) fewer harms than they would with existing care. The central proof gap is the distance between a treatment being new and a treatment being demonstrably superior.

This distinction matters because modern regulatory systems often allow promising therapies to reach patients before every question has been answered. Early access can save lives, particularly for serious diseases with limited options. At the same time, history shows that some interventions that looked promising at first later failed to deliver meaningful patient benefits when tested more rigorously. The logical error occurs when “newly available” is treated as evidence of “better than current care” without the comparisons needed to support that conclusion. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/table-surrogate-endpoints-were-basis-drug-approval-or-licensure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fda.gov">[U.S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fda.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">table surrogate endpoints were basis drug approval or licensure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Table of Surrogate Endpoints That Were the Basis of Drug...29 Apr 2026 — This surrogate endpoint table includes surrogate endpoints that...</span></span></span>. ## Why Approval Is Not the Same as Proven Superiority

Many people assume that regulatory approval means a treatment has been proven better than existing alternatives. In reality, approval and superiority are different claims.

Approval generally means regulators have concluded that a product's benefits outweigh its risks for a specific use based on available evidence. In some circumstances, especially for serious diseases, approval may be granted using evidence that predicts benefit rather than directly demonstrating it. The US Food and Drug Administration's accelerated approval pathway, for example, allows approval based on a surrogate endpoint—a laboratory measurement, imaging result, or other marker thought to predict patient benefit but not itself a direct measure of how patients feel, function, or survive. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/table-surrogate-endpoints-were-basis-drug-approval-or-licensure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fda.gov">[U.S]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fda.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">table surrogate endpoints were basis drug approval or licensure</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Table of Surrogate Endpoints That Were the Basis of Drug...29 Apr 2026 — This surrogate endpoint table includes surrogate endpoints that...</span></span></span>. A cancer drug illustrates the distinction. If a treatment shrinks tumours, that may be encouraging. However, tumour shrinkage is not identical to proving that patients live longer or enjoy a better quality of life. The treatment may eventually show those benefits, but the evidence must still be gathered. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/accelerated-approval-confirmatory-trials-requirement-may-further-complicate-life" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whitecase.com">[White &amp; Case]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whitecase.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">accelerated approval confirmatory trials requirement may further complicate life</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">White &amp; CaseAccelerated Approval confirmatory trials requirement may...14 Aug 2025 — Accelerated Approval allows the FDA to approve drug...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.center4research.org/accelerated-approval-pathway-summary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: center4research.org">[National Center for Health Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">center4research.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Center for Health ResearchFDA Accelerated Approval Pathway: Controversies and...Accelerated approval is usually based on change...</span></span></span>

The appeal to [novelty]({{ 'novelty/' | relative_url }}) enters when advocates move from “this treatment has been approved” to “this treatment is therefore better than standard care”. Approval may justify access. It does not automatically establish superiority.

### The Problem of Surrogate Measures

Medicine frequently relies on measurements that can be observed earlier than final patient outcomes. Blood pressure, cholesterol levels, tumour response, and laboratory markers can all provide useful information. ## How Trials, Reversals, and Standard-Care Comparisons Change the Claim

The strongest evidence that a new treatment is genuinely better usually comes from direct comparison with the current standard of care.

A treatment may appear impressive in an early study if patients improve after receiving it. Yet many illnesses fluctuate naturally, and patients often improve for reasons unrelated to the intervention. Randomised controlled trials help address these problems by comparing outcomes between groups and reducing bias.

The key question is not whether the new treatment works in isolation. The key question is whether it works better than what patients would otherwise receive.

### Why Direct Comparisons Matter

A new therapy can outperform a placebo while still offering little advantage over established treatment. Conversely, it may provide similar effectiveness but fewer side effects, lower costs, or greater convenience. Without direct comparison, claims of superiority remain speculative. Regulatory and scientific guidance frequently treats active-comparator superiority trials as particularly strong evidence when the objective is to demonstrate that a new option improves on existing care. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11000139/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clinical Benefit and Regulatory Outcomes of Cancer Drugs...by ITT Liu · 2024 · Cited by 135 — The US Food and Drug Administration&#x27;s (...</span></span></span>

This is where many new-is-better claims become vulnerable. Marketing language often highlights innovation, mechanism, or technological sophistication, while the more important comparative questions receive less attention.

### Medical Reversals: When Better Evidence Changes the Story

Medicine also provides a cautionary record of interventions that initially appeared beneficial but later failed under stronger testing.

Researchers use the term *medical reversal* for situations in which a widely adopted practice is later shown through better evidence to be ineffective or less beneficial than previously believed. Studies examining the medical literature have identified hundreds of such reversals across different areas of healthcare. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nber.org/sites/default/files/2022-09/WhitePaper-Bolder2.2019.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nber.org">[NBER]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nber.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Introduction to a New...by H Bolder · 2019 · Cited by 1 — A “medical reversal” occurs when new evidence (usually from a high-quality RCT...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11000139/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clinical Benefit and Regulatory Outcomes of Cancer Drugs...by ITT Liu · 2024 · Cited by 135 — The US Food and Drug Administration&#x27;s (...</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.mayoclinicproceedings.org/article/s0025-6196%2813%2900405-9/fulltext" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mayoclinicproceedings.org">[Mayo Clinic Proceedings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mayoclinicproceedings.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">This investigation sheds light on low-value practices.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mayo Clinic ProceedingsA Decade of Reversal: An Analysis of 146 Contradicted...by V Prasad · 2013 · Cited by 470 — The reversal of estab...</span></span></span>

The existence of medical reversals does not mean that medical progress is an illusion. In fact, reversals demonstrate science working as intended: claims are continuously tested against stronger evidence. The lesson for the appeal to novelty is different. Early enthusiasm is not proof. A promising mechanism, exciting preliminary result, or new technology can all fail when subjected to rigorous comparison. ## The Real Test of Medical Progress

The history of medicine shows both remarkable innovation and recurring episodes of overconfidence. New treatments have transformed survival for many diseases, but they have done so because evidence eventually demonstrated meaningful benefits, not because they were new.

Within the appeal to novelty fallacy, the proof gap is the critical issue. Novelty can justify attention, investment, and further study. It cannot by itself justify the conclusion that a treatment is better than established care. In medicine, the decisive question is not whether an intervention is the latest option available. It is whether careful comparison shows that patients are actually better off because of it.
